Leaving Your Dog Home Alone While You’re at Work

How long is too long, how to set your dog up for a calm workday, and when a midday potty break makes all the difference. A practical guide for busy dog owners in Frisco, Prosper, Celina, and Little Elm.

By Paws in Stride · Local dog walkers & pet sitters in Frisco, TX

The quick answer

Most healthy adult dogs do best with a potty break at least every 4–6 hours. Puppies and senior dogs need breaks more often. If your workday plus commute keeps your dog alone for 8+ hours, a midday walk or drop-in visit keeps them comfortable, less bored, and far less likely to have accidents.

How long can a dog be left home alone?

There’s no single number that fits every dog. Age, size, health, and energy level all matter. These are common, conservative guidelines many trainers and vets use as a starting point:

DogComfortable time aloneWhy
Puppy (2–3 months)1–2 hoursTiny bladders and still learning potty habits
Puppy (4–6 months)2–4 hoursA popular rule of thumb: about one hour per month of age, plus one
Adult dog4–6 hoursComfortable for most; 8 hours is a long stretch without a break
Senior dog2–6 hoursOften need to go out more often, and may have health needs

Every dog is different. If your dog has a medical condition, takes medication, or is newly adopted, ask your veterinarian what’s right for them.

Here’s the catch for most working owners: an 8-hour workday plus a commute in DFW traffic often means 9–10 hours away from home. Even a dog who can physically “hold it” that long is usually bored, restless, and uncomfortable by the end of the day.

Signs your dog is struggling while you’re gone

Dogs can’t tell us their day was too long, but they show it. Watch for:

  • Accidents in the house, especially from a dog who is otherwise house-trained
  • Chewing, digging, or scratching at doors, blinds, furniture, or crates
  • Barking or howling (neighbors, apartment managers, or your HOA may mention it before you notice)
  • Over-the-top greetings or nonstop zoomies the second you walk in
  • Restlessness at night because they never burned off their energy during the day
  • Drooling, pacing, or panting when you get ready to leave, which can point to separation anxiety

If you’re seeing these regularly, it usually means your dog needs one of two things: a break in the middle of the day, or more exercise before you leave. Often it’s both.

How to set your dog up for a good workday

1

Exercise before you leave

A tired dog is a calm dog. Even a 20–30 minute morning walk or a game of fetch helps your dog settle and nap while you’re gone.

2

Potty break right before you walk out the door

Make it the very last thing you do, so your dog starts the day with an empty bladder.

3

Give them a safe, comfortable space

Some dogs love a crate; others do better in a dog-proofed room or behind a baby gate. Crates are great for short stretches, but most adult dogs shouldn’t be crated for an entire workday without a break.

4

Leave something to do

Puzzle feeders, lick mats, and frozen stuffed toys keep minds busy for the first hour, which is often when anxious dogs struggle most.

5

Always leave fresh water, and keep the A/C on

Never turn the A/C off for your dog’s workday in Texas. Homes heat up fast, especially upstairs rooms and spaces with big windows.

6

Keep departures calm and boring

Skip the long, emotional goodbye. A calm exit (and a calm return) teaches your dog that you leaving isn’t a big deal.

7

Dog-proof the space

Put away shoes, cords, trash, medications, and anything chewable or toxic. Close doors to rooms they shouldn’t explore.

8

Consider a pet camera

Seeing what your dog actually does all day (sleep? pace? bark?) is the fastest way to know whether they need more support.

Frisco-specific things to plan for

☀️ Texas heat

Summer afternoons in North Texas can be brutal. Midday potty breaks should be quick and shaded, and hot pavement can burn paws. If you can’t comfortably hold the back of your hand on the sidewalk for 7 seconds, it’s too hot for paws.

⛈️ Spring storms

Thunder and severe weather can be scary for dogs home alone. A cozy interior room, background noise, and a midday check-in can help nervous pups on stormy days.

🏢 Apartments & HOAs

Barking complaints are common in apartments and close-together neighborhoods. A midday break that burns energy is one of the easiest ways to keep things quiet.

🚗 Long commutes

A “quick” trip down the Tollway or 121 can easily add an hour or more to your day, and your dog feels every minute of it.

When a midday potty break helps most

A midday visit breaks a long day into two short ones. Your dog gets relief, fresh air, attention, and a reset, and you get peace of mind with photo updates sent right to your phone.

A midday drop-in visit is a great fit if:

  • You’re away for 8 hours or more (including your commute)
  • You have a puppy who can’t hold it long yet
  • You have a senior dog who needs to go out more often
  • Your dog needs a midday meal or medication
  • You’re noticing accidents, chewing, or barking while you’re gone

A sample workday schedule

Here’s what a balanced day can look like for an adult dog with a full-time working owner:

  1. 6:45 AM Morning walk or play session
  2. 7:15 AM Breakfast, then a calm settle-down
  3. 7:45 AM Last potty break, puzzle toy, and a low-key goodbye
  4. 12:00 PM Midday drop-in: potty break, fresh water, play, and photo updates
  5. 5:30 PM You’re home: evening walk and dinner
  6. 9:30 PM Last potty break before bed

That one midday visit turns a 10-hour stretch into two easy 4–5 hour stretches.

Frequently asked questions

Is it okay to leave my dog home alone for 8 hours?

Some adult dogs can manage it occasionally, but 8 hours (plus a commute) is a long time without a potty break, company, or exercise. For an everyday routine, most dogs are much happier with a midday break.

Should I crate my dog while I’m at work?

Crates can be a safe, cozy space, but most adult dogs shouldn’t spend an entire workday crated without a break. If your dog is crated, a midday visit to let them out to potty and stretch makes a big difference.

What’s the difference between a drop-in visit and a dog walk?

A drop-in visit focuses on a potty break, fresh water, feeding, and some playtime at home. A dog walk is focused on exercise out in the neighborhood and includes a GPS-tracked route. Both include photo updates.

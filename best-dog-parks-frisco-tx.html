8 Best Dog-Friendly Parks & Walking Trails in Frisco, TX

By Paws in Stride · Updated September 2026

Frisco has no shortage of places to get outside with your dog. From off-leash dog parks to shaded walking trails, nature preserves, and neighborhood green spaces, there are plenty of options when your pup needs exercise and a change of scenery.

As a local dog walking company serving Frisco, we spend a lot of time outdoors with dogs throughout the area. Below are 8 Frisco parks and trails worth checking out with your four-legged companion.

The short answer

For off-leash play, start with Northwest Community Park, Frisco’s newest dog park (opened September 2026), then Ruff Range Dog Park, the City’s longtime dedicated dog park, or the Hall Park Dog Park next to Kaleidoscope Park. Everything else on this list is a leash-only park, trail, or nature area.

Before you go: not every place on this list is an off-leash dog park. Frisco requires dogs to be restrained by a leash or other physical restraint at all times in city parks, except inside a designated off-leash area. Dogs aren’t allowed in or around athletic fields, concessions, restrooms, plazas, or playground areas, or within 75 feet of them. Always follow posted signs and park rules.

  1. 1

    Northwest Community Park

    13770 Teel Parkway, Frisco, TX 75033Directions

    Off-leash dog park

    Northwest Community Park is the newest addition to this list — it had its grand opening on September 19, 2026, and includes a dedicated 5-acre dog park as part of its 122 acres of restored Blackland Prairie.

    Like Ruff Range, the dog park here has separate off-leash areas for small dogs (35 pounds and under) and large dogs (over 35 pounds). The rest of the park has over 10 miles of trails, three playgrounds (including a 20-foot bison play structure), a splash pad, mini zip lines, and a 20-acre open lawn, so it's an easy park to spend a whole morning at.

    Why dog owners may like it

    Brand new, spacious, and separated by dog size, with plenty to do nearby once your dog is worn out.

    Good for

    Social dogs that enjoy off-leash exercise, and families who want a dog park plus trails and playgrounds in one trip.

    Local tip: Since this park just opened, expect it to be busy on weekends while people check it out. Frisco requires a leash everywhere outside the designated dog park, and dogs must stay at least 75 feet from playgrounds, athletic fields, and plazas — so keep that in mind if you're walking through the rest of the park.

  2. 2

    Ruff Range Dog Park

    5335 4th Army Memorial Drive, Frisco, TX 75034Directions

    Off-leash dog park

    If you’re specifically looking for an off-leash dog park in Frisco, Ruff Range should be at the top of your list.

    It sits on the south side of B.F. Phillips Community Park and has separate off-leash areas for large dogs (over 35 pounds) and small dogs (35 pounds and under), plus pavilions and seating, water stations, dog-wash stations, and parking. There are two large-dog areas, and the City keeps only one open at a time so the other can recover, switching roughly every two weeks.

    Why dog owners may like it

    Space to run, separate areas by dog size, water access, and facilities designed for dogs.

    Good for

    Social dogs that enjoy off-leash exercise.

    Local tip: The City closes Ruff Range every Wednesday for maintenance, and it turns the park’s water fountains off from December through March. The park also has its own rules (for example, always carry a leash and no food or treats inside), so read them before your first visit and check the City’s Ruff Range page for current conditions.

  3. 3

    Hall Park Dog Park

    6635 Warren Parkway, Frisco, TX 75034Directions

    Dog park

    Hall Park Dog Park sits right next to Kaleidoscope Park, one of Frisco’s newest destinations, which opened in 2024 in the HALL Park district near the Dallas North Tollway and Warren Parkway.

    Beyond the dog park, the six-acre park has public art, gardens, a playground, a splash pad, and performance lawns, which makes it a fun choice if you want to combine your dog’s outing with a day around Frisco.

    Why dog owners may like it

    A modern setting, a dedicated dog area, and plenty happening nearby.

    Good for

    Dogs that enjoy social outings, and owners who want more than a traditional neighborhood park.

    Local tip: The park is open daily from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m., and parking is in the garage at 6605 Warren Parkway, where the first two hours are free. Rules for dogs elsewhere in the park may be stricter than in the dog park itself, so check the posted rules and the park’s FAQ.

  4. 4

    Frisco Commons Park

    8000 McKinney Road, Frisco, TX 75034Directions

    Leash-only park

    Frisco Commons covers about 63 acres and has a hike-and-bike trail, a fishing pond, playgrounds, a splash pad, open meadow space, and parking.

    For dog owners, the walking paths and large grounds make it a good choice when you want a more structured leash walk instead of off-leash play.

    Why dog owners may like it

    Plenty of space, established walking paths, and a mix of open and landscaped areas.

    Good for

    Longer walks, lower-key dogs, and leash training.

  5. 5

    Limestone Quarry Park & Taychas Trail

    1230 Maltby Drive, Frisco, TX 75035Directions

    Leash-only trail

    This is one of the more interesting nature walks in Frisco. Limestone Quarry Park is a 17-acre neighborhood park with wildflowers, native grasses, mosaic artwork, and creek scenery.

    It connects to the Taychas Trail, which Visit Frisco describes as a 2.2-mile trail that runs north from the park, so you can keep going for a longer walk.

    Why dog owners may like it

    Lots of smells and scenery for dogs that enjoy exploring rather than walking the same neighborhood loop.

    Good for

    Sniff-heavy walks and dogs that enjoy nature.

    Local tip: The park has a playground, and city rules keep dogs out of playground areas and the 75 feet around them. Stick to the trail and follow the posted signs.

  6. 6

    Cottonwood Creek Greenbelt

    3925 Bear Creek Lane, Frisco, TX 75033Directions

    Nature trail

    For a quieter nature walk, Cottonwood Creek Greenbelt is hard to beat. The greenbelt covers 77 acres and has almost two miles of trail, wetlands, ponds, wildlife-viewing platforms, and parking.

    The City keeps it as natural habitat, so it feels less manicured than a typical park, and that can make an ordinary dog walk much more stimulating.

    Why dog owners may like it

    A longer trail, natural scenery, ponds, wildlife, and plenty for dogs to smell.

    Good for

    Active dogs and longer leash walks.

  7. 7

    J.C. Grant Park & Caddo Trail

    8220 Wade Boulevard, Frisco, TXDirections

    Park & trail

    J.C. Grant Park is a 9-acre park next to Spears Elementary. It connects to the Caddo Trail, a 1.1-mile trail that follows Stewart Creek south of Stonebrook Parkway and runs through the Chapel Creek neighborhood.

    It works well when your dog needs a decent walk but you don’t want to spend an entire afternoon at a large park.

    Why dog owners may like it

    Creekside scenery, a trail connection, and a convenient distance for everyday walking.

    Good for

    Moderate exercise and routine leash walks.

  8. 8

    Big Bluestem Trail at Grand Park

    5001 Cotton Gin Road, Frisco, TX 75034Directions

    Nature trail

    Big Bluestem Trail is a natural, unpaved 2.2-mile trail that winds through the undeveloped land at Grand Park. Parking and the trailhead are off Cotton Gin Road, between Legacy Drive and Dallas Parkway, and the trail runs through Grand Park to Stonebrook Parkway.

    The City also points to the Wildflower Loop along the trail as a spot to enjoy native blooms, so it’s worth a visit in spring and fall.

    Why dog owners may like it

    A nature-focused walk with plenty of opportunities for sniffing and exploring.

    Good for

    Active dogs and owners who want to mix up their normal neighborhood walk.

Frisco dog park questions

What is the best off-leash dog park in Frisco?

If you’re specifically looking for off-leash play, start with the new Northwest Community Park dog park, Ruff Range Dog Park, or Hall Park Dog Park next to Kaleidoscope Park. If your goal is exercise, enrichment, or a longer walk, Frisco’s trail system gives you many more options. The City maintains more than 60 parks along with a growing network of trails and natural areas.

Are dogs allowed off leash in Frisco parks?

Only inside designated off-leash areas like the dog parks. Everywhere else in Frisco’s parks and trails, dogs need to be accompanied by a responsible person and restrained by a leash or other physical restraint at all times.

Do I have to pick up after my dog?

Yes. City ordinance makes owners responsible for picking up after their dogs and disposing of the waste properly.

How do I keep my dog safe in the North Texas heat?

North Texas heat is no joke, especially in summer. On hot days, walk earlier in the morning or later in the evening, bring water, watch your dog’s breathing and energy level, and be careful with hot pavement.
